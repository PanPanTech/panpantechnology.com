AHR-1636 Wheeled Dual-Arm Humanoid Robot

The AHR-1636 is a 1,636 mm wheeled humanoid robot for retail service, light manufacturing, logistics and research. Two 7-DoF arms rated 10 kg each, dexterous five-finger hands or two-finger grippers, a human-like stereo vision head and up to 40 degrees of freedom let it work at counters, shelves and workbenches built for people. A simulation-trained AI stack and an open ROS 2 SDK shorten the path from a new task to a working deployment.

What it solves

A robot that fits workplaces built for people

/01
Works where people work

Human proportions, two arms and a 1,636 mm height let it use existing counters, shelves and workbenches instead of a site rebuilt around fixed automation.

/02
One body, many tasks

Hands or grippers, humanoid or industrial-style arms, differential or omnidirectional base and single or dual PC are configured to the job, so the same platform can greet, fetch, pick and assemble.

/03
Faster to new tasks

Training data is generated in simulation — lighting, friction, rigid and fluid dynamics — and transferred to the real robot. The manufacturer reports around 6,000 training samples a day this way, against 100–300 from teleoperation and manual labelling.

/04
Precise, gentle force control

Whole-body joints run at 1 kHz over an EtherCAT network with 0.1 N force sensitivity, and both arms support compliant control for safe contact with objects and people.

/05
Human-like stereo vision

A 6 cm-baseline stereo head with a large-model depth algorithm and microsecond multi-camera sync sees in changing light; wrist cameras are optional for close-range picking.

/06
Open for developers

ROS 2-based, with joint-level force/position control for researchers and drag teaching, impedance mode and industrial motion commands for application builders.

How it works

Body, eyes, brain and an open software stack

The hardware is designed and checked in simulation first, so the robot that arrives on site already matches the training environment its skills were learned in.

01

Humanoid body

34 degrees of freedom on the differential base or 40 with the omnidirectional base: two 7-DoF arms, a 2-DoF neck, 2-DoF waist and 2-DoF legs, with all cabling run inside hollow joints.

02

Stereo vision + SLAM

Humanoid stereo vision with a 90° × 60° field of view, plus laser and visual SLAM navigation repeatable to ±10 mm.

03

Simulation-trained skills

Synthetic data and auto-labelled scenes train the base model; a single human demonstration can be turned into a precise dual-arm trajectory without a teleoperation rig.

04

Layered open software

UI, API and voice on top; task planning with behaviour trees, state machines or LLM/VLM; manipulation, perception, navigation and VLA algorithms; an SDK over EtherCAT, Ethernet, USB, CAN and RS-485.

Deployment

From a task video to a running pilot

Most projects start with one well-defined task and a short supervised pilot before more skills are added.

STEP 01

Task & site review

A video of the task, the objects and their weights, counter or shelf heights, aisle widths, network coverage and where the robot will charge.

STEP 02

Configuration

Dexterous hand or gripper, differential or omnidirectional base, single or dual PC and optional wrist cameras are chosen for the task.

STEP 03

Train & validate

The task is modelled in simulation, training data is generated, and demonstrations or teleoperation fill the gaps before the robot touches real stock.

STEP 04

Pilot & scale

A supervised pilot on site, then OTA skill updates, automatic charging and additional tasks once the first one is stable.

AHR-1636 FAQ

Questions buyers ask about wheeled humanoid robots

Does the AHR-1636 walk on legs?
No. It has a humanoid upper body on a wheeled base. Wheels give it a stable platform for two-handed work, a top speed of 1.5 m/s and a runtime of 8 hours or more, which matters more in shops, labs and factories than walking.
How much can it lift?
Each 7-DoF arm is rated at 10 kg at 0.5 m from the shoulder. For heavier parts or longer reach at a machine, the AMC-0010 mobile cobot or an AMR is the better fit.
How long does it take to teach it a new task?
It depends on the task. Simulation-generated training data, one-shot learning from a human demonstration, drag teaching and teleoperation all shorten the process compared with collecting real-world data from scratch. We recommend a supervised pilot on the real task before committing to a rollout.
Should I choose dexterous hands or two-finger grippers?
Dexterous five-finger hands (6 active DoF each) suit varied objects and human-facing service such as handing items to customers. Two-finger grippers are simpler and suit repeatable industrial picks. The choice is confirmed after we see your objects.
Can our own developers program it?
Yes. The software stack is ROS 2-based, with joint-level force/position hybrid control for researchers and high-level APIs, drag teaching, impedance mode and industrial motion commands for application developers. Task planning can use behaviour trees, state machines or LLM/VLM planners.
Where does it work best?
At counters, shelves and workbenches designed for people: convenience retail, reception and guided shopping, light assembly and sorting, warehouse picking pilots, research labs and service trials. Floors, slopes, network coverage and charging position are checked during the task review.
How is it different from the ASR-0012?
The ASR-0012 is a purpose-built retail robot with a 3 kg arm and zero-shot product recognition for stock checks, restocking and order picking. The AHR-1636 is a general-purpose two-armed humanoid with 10 kg arms for a wider range of service and light industrial tasks.
What documentation is available?
Configuration, certification coverage for your market and the technical documentation are confirmed in the quotation, together with training and support terms.
