How much does a commercial cleaning robot cost?

In 2026, published list prices put entry-level robotic scrubbers at roughly $15,000–$25,000, mainstream commercial autonomous scrubbers at $25,000–$45,000, and large industrial cleaning robots at $40,000–$70,000 per unit. Robot-as-a-Service (RaaS) subscriptions typically run $600–$2,000 per month with service included.

Those bands come from US reseller pricing guides published this year (for example Mobotics' 2026 robot floor scrubber cost guide), and they line up with the list prices resellers show for named models, which we tabulate below. Treat them as a starting point, not a quote: the same robot can differ by thousands of dollars between regions, channels and configurations.

The more useful question is what moves a robot from the bottom of its band to the top. In practice, four things explain most of the difference: the robot's class (how big a floor it is built for), how much the docking station automates, the sensor suite, and what is bundled for service and software. The rest of this guide walks through each one, then shows how to turn a price into a payback period.

Two patterns stand out. First, the docking station is often a separate line item, so two quotes for the same robot may not include the same equipment. What drives the price of a cleaning robot

Size and station automation drive most of the price. Sensors and software come next. Brand and channel decide the last 10–30%.
→Machine class. A 600–800 mm scrub path, 60–140 L tanks and a 4–6 hour battery cost far more to build than a 350 mm compact robot. Size the class to your floor first; it is the biggest price lever.
→Station automation. A charge-only dock is cheap. A station that refills, drains, doses detergent and washes the robot removes daily labour, and costs accordingly. A station plumbed to water and drain, like the one on our ACR-0520P, is what makes 24/7 unattended cleaning possible.
→Sensors. 3D LiDAR, depth cameras and AI stain recognition cost more than single-line LiDAR. They matter in busy public spaces; in a quiet warehouse aisle they may not.
→Cleaning functions. A robot that only vacuums is simpler than a 4-in-1 that sweeps, vacuums, scrubs and mops, and far simpler than a 6-in-1 with self-cleaning and sanitising.
→Software and fleet management. Some suppliers bundle the cloud platform; others charge a yearly subscription. Ask for it in writing.
→Certifications and documentation. CE, FCC, battery transport and safety documents for your market add cost but are not optional. See our guide to cleaning robot certifications.
→Channel. A local reseller's price includes demonstration, installation and first-line service. Factory-direct and distributor pricing is structured differently, with service delivered through a partner.

Consumables are the item buyers most often forget. Squeegee blades and brushes on a robot that runs every night wear like those on any scrubber. Ask for a consumables price list before you sign, and multiply it by your planned hours.

Buy, lease or subscribe?

Buy if you have capital and in-house facility staff; lease if you want to spread cost but keep the asset; choose RaaS if you want a fixed monthly fee with service included and the option to swap models.

Contract cleaners often prefer RaaS because the cost maps cleanly to a client contract. Owners of large sites usually buy, because over five years ownership is cheaper if the robot keeps running.

From price to payback: a worked example

A robot pays back when the labour hours it takes over are worth more than its yearly cost. With US janitor wages at a $17.27 median per hour (BLS, May 2024), a robot that reliably takes over three hours of floor work a night is offsetting about $18,900 a year in wages alone.

Here is the arithmetic, using the US Bureau of Labor Statistics median wage for janitors and building cleaners:

→Labour offset: 3 hours × 365 nights × $17.27 = $18,911 per year, before payroll taxes and benefits.
→Supervision: subtract the time someone still spends starting routes, emptying bins and spot-checking. At 20 minutes a night that is about $2,100 per year.
→Robot cost per year: a $35,000 robot over five years is $7,000 a year, plus consumables and service, say $3,000.
→Net saving: $18,911 − $2,100 − $10,000 ≈ $6,800 per year after write-off. In cash terms the $35,000 purchase is recovered from $13,800 a year of savings after running costs, a payback of about 2.5 years on wages alone.

The sensitive inputs are hours taken over and supervision time, not the robot price. A robot that takes over one hour a night will struggle to pay back; one that takes over six will pay back fast. Run your own numbers in our robot floor scrubber ROI calculator.

How to get an accurate quote

Send floor area by type, cleaning frequency, the narrowest aisle, soil type, whether a station can connect to water, and your country. With that, a supplier can quote the right class instead of a catalogue price.
→Floor area and type — square metres of each finish: sealed concrete, vinyl, tile, carpet.
→Frequency and window — how often each area is cleaned, and when it is empty.
→Narrowest route — aisle and door widths decide the robot size.
→Soil — dust, liquid spills, tyre marks, grease or textile scraps.
→Utilities — whether a station can connect to water and drain, or only to power.
→Destination and channel — country of use, certifications needed, and whether you buy, lease or resell.

FAQ

How much does a commercial cleaning robot cost?
In 2026, entry-level robotic scrubbers list for roughly $15,000–$25,000, mainstream commercial autonomous scrubbers for $25,000–$45,000, and large industrial cleaning robots for $40,000–$70,000. RaaS subscriptions typically run $600–$2,000 per month.
What is the cheapest commercial cleaning robot?
Compact robots for small floors are at the bottom of the range, often under $25,000. They suit shops, offices and restaurants but run out of tank and battery on large floors, so a cheap robot on the wrong floor costs more in the end.
Is the docking station included in the price?
Not always. Some resellers price the station separately, and self-cleaning stations cost more than charge-only docks. Ask for robot, station, software and warranty as one line.
Is it cheaper to rent a cleaning robot?
Monthly cost is lower, but total cost over the term is usually higher than buying. RaaS makes sense when you want service included, need to match a client contract, or are not sure which model suits you.
How long does a cleaning robot take to pay back?
Typically one to three years when the robot takes over several hours of floor work a day. Payback depends far more on hours taken over and supervision time than on the purchase price.
What ongoing costs should I budget for?
Consumables (brushes, pads, squeegee blades, filters), service after warranty, software subscriptions where they apply, and a mid-life battery replacement.
Can I buy direct from a manufacturer?
Yes. Manufacturers such as PanPanTech sell to distributors, integrators and large end users, and support OEM and private-label programmes. Service is then delivered through the distributor or a local partner.
