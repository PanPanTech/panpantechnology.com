Heavy-Payload AMRs: How to Choose a 600 kg Robot for Racks, Carts and Pallets

A 600 kg AMR is the right class when you move loaded racks, heavy trolleys or pallets on stands between fixed points, and one 300 kg robot would need two trips. Choose by how the robot carries the load — driving under it and lifting, carrying it on a top module, or towing it — then check the centre of gravity, the floor, thresholds, lifts and charging. For full pallets lifted straight off the floor, look at pallet AMRs and autonomous forklifts instead.

Short answer: match the carrying method first, then the kilograms

Pick a 600 kg AMR when your heaviest regular load is between roughly 300 and 600 kg and travels on a rack, trolley or stand that a robot can drive under, lift or tow. Pick an underride lifting robot for racks and shelves, a mast or top-module robot for conveyor and line-side transfer, and a towing robot for carts in trains. If the job is lifting bare pallets from the floor or racking, you need a pallet AMR or autonomous forklift, not a 600 kg mobile robot.

Heavy-payload AMRs exist for one economic reason: fewer trips. When a 300 kg robot has to split a load into two runs, a 600 kg robot halves the traffic in the aisle and the queue at the station. But the payload rating is the last thing to check, not the first. A robot rated for 600 kg can still be the wrong machine if your racks are too tall, your floor has steps it cannot cross, or your lift cannot take it.

This guide covers how heavy AMRs carry loads, what the rating does not tell you, what fits on a 600 kg robot, the site and safety checks, and how our own AMR-0600 and AMR-0600R compare with the 300 kg class. If you are still deciding between AMRs and guided vehicles, start with AMR vs AGV.

AMR payload classes: where 600 kg sits

Suppliers draw the lines differently, but most mobile robots for material handling fall into four broad bands. The band decides the chassis, the lifting or towing mechanism, the battery and, above all, the safety case.

ClassTypical loadsTypical robot
Light, up to about 100 kgTotes, parcels, trays, hospital and hotel deliveriesDelivery robots with shelves or lockers
Medium, about 300 kgSmall racks, kitting carts, line-side bins, conveyor transfersIndustrial AMRs with lifting, conveyor or towing tops
Heavy, about 600 kgLoaded racks and shelves, heavy trolleys, pallets on stands, roll cagesUnderride lifting AMRs and heavy mast AMRs
Very heavy, 1,000 kg and aboveFull pallets, engine and press tooling, coils, large assembliesPallet AMRs, autonomous forklifts, heavy AGVs

The step from 300 to 600 kg is usually the cheapest way to cut trips without changing how the site works, because the same racks and trolleys stay in use. The step beyond 1,000 kg usually changes the whole handling method — pallets instead of racks, forks instead of lifting tops — and with it the safety case and the floor requirements.

Four ways a heavy AMR carries a load

Underride lifting robots drive under a rack and raise it; mast or top-module robots carry the load on top; towing robots pull carts behind them; fork-type robots lift pallets. The first three are what the 600 kg class does best.

Underride robots stay compact and turn on the spot, which suits dense rack areas: the AMR-0600R is 365 mm high with a 50 mm lifting stroke, and the underride version of the AMR-0600 is just 255 mm high. Mast versions keep a screen and controls on the robot, so they can run without a central control system, and they accept conveyor and roller tops. For cart trains, the AMR-0300 towing version is rated to pull 400 kg.

What the 600 kg rating does not tell you

A payload rating is measured under defined conditions: a centred load, a flat floor and specified acceleration and braking. Your loads and floors will not match those conditions exactly, so ask for the payload envelope, not just the headline number.

Centre of gravityA tall rack with heavy items on the top shelf can tip under braking long before it reaches 600 kg. Ask for the maximum load height and centre-of-gravity limits, and load heavy items low.
Acceleration and brakingStopping distance grows with mass. Heavy robots need lower speeds in shared zones; check that the safety-field settings are validated with the full load, not empty.
RampsUnderride robots carry the load above the chassis, so slopes are limited — the AMR-0300R, for example, is rated for 5° over a 10 m run. Ask for gradeability at full load.
Steps and gapsHeavy robots often cross smaller steps than light ones: 10 mm on the AMR-0600 against 20 mm on the AMR-0300. Both cross 35 mm gaps. Measure every threshold, expansion joint and lift sill on the route.
Run-timePublished run-times are usually unloaded — 12 h for the AMR-0600 — and fall with load, ramps and stop-start traffic. Size charging on your real duty cycle.

Racks, carts and pallets: what fits on a 600 kg robot

Racks and shelves

Underride robots need a rack whose legs leave room for the robot to drive under and whose frame takes the lifting load at the centre. Standard racks are often supplied with the robot — 920 × 860 × 423 mm outer size for the AMR-0600R and 660 × 700 × 350 mm for the AMR-0300R — and custom racks are common. If you plan to reuse existing racks, measure leg spacing, under-clearance and frame strength before ordering.

Trolleys and carts

Heavy trolleys can be lifted from underneath or towed. Lifting keeps the footprint small and the robot in control of the load; towing moves several carts at once but needs turning space and carts with good castors and brakes. Many sites use both: underride robots in dense storage, towing robots on long milk-run routes.

Pallets

A 600 kg AMR can move pallets when the pallet sits on a stand or rack the robot drives under; the AMR-0600R is described for exactly this kind of pallet-rack transfer. It cannot pick a bare pallet off the floor or out of racking — that needs forks. If most of your flows are floor-level pallets above 1,000 kg, a pallet AMR or autonomous forklift is the right comparison, and the extra cost of stands for a 600 kg robot will not pay back.

Safety with heavy loads

Every industrial AMR falls under ISO 3691-4, the safety standard for driverless industrial trucks, and in North America under ANSI/A3 R15.08. A heavier load does not change the standard, but it changes the risk assessment: longer stopping distances, more energy in a collision, more serious consequences if a rack tips or a load falls.

→Validate safety fields and speed zones with the robot fully loaded, not empty.
→Set lower speeds where people walk, at blind corners and at stations.
→Define load rules: maximum height, heavy items low, how loads are secured on the rack or cart.
→Check physical protection: bumpers and emergency stops (the AMR-0600R has front and rear bumpers and two e-stops).
→Plan manual recovery: how staff move a stopped robot carrying 600 kg, and who is trained to do it.

Fleet software and integration

Heavy robots are usually few in number but busy, so scheduling matters more than raw speed. Three features make the most difference. Rack recognition lets the robot find and pick up the right rack itself; the AMR-0600 identifies target storage locations and performs pick-and-place autonomously. Lift scheduling keeps heavy robots from blocking lifts; the AMR-0600 calls idle lifts first. Open interfaces avoid lock-in: the AMR-0600 supports VDA 5050 for mixed fleets and third-party fleet managers, and can be deployed on-premises when data has to stay on site.

Start a pilot with call buttons or manual dispatch, then connect to the WMS or MES once routes are stable. Our fleet cloud platform handles dispatch, maps and reporting for our own robots.

FAQ

What is a heavy-payload AMR?
An autonomous mobile robot built to move loads of several hundred kilograms — typically around 600 kg — such as loaded racks, heavy trolleys and pallets on stands. It navigates by itself like a lighter AMR but has a stronger chassis, lifting or towing mechanism and a safety case validated for the heavier load.
How much weight can an AMR carry?
It depends on the class: delivery robots carry up to about 100 kg, industrial AMRs about 300 kg, heavy-payload AMRs about 600 kg, and pallet AMRs and autonomous forklifts 1,000 kg and more. Our AMR-0600 and AMR-0600R are rated for 600 kg; the AMR-0300 tows up to 400 kg.
Can a 600 kg AMR move pallets?
Yes, when the pallet sits on a stand or rack the robot can drive under and lift. It cannot pick a bare pallet from the floor or from racking; that needs a pallet AMR or autonomous forklift with forks.
What is the difference between an underride lifting AMR and a towing AMR?
An underride robot drives beneath a rack or trolley, lifts it a few centimetres and carries it, staying compact and able to turn on the spot. A towing robot hitches carts and pulls them, moving several at once but needing more turning space and carts with good castors and brakes.
How long does a 600 kg AMR run on a charge?
Published figures are usually unloaded: 12 hours for the AMR-0600 and 8 hours for the AMR-0600R. Real run-time falls with load, ramps and stop-start traffic, so plan charging around your duty cycle; the AMR-0600 reaches 90 % in 2 hours and the AMR-0600R charges fully in about 1.1 hours.
What aisle width does a 600 kg AMR need?
The robot's path clearance plus the width of the load and a safety margin. The AMR-0600 needs 700 mm path clearance in its mast version and 650 mm as an underride robot; wide racks need more, and two-way traffic needs room for two loaded robots or a single-lane rule.
Can heavy AMRs use lifts and ramps?
Lifts, yes, if the lift is rated for robot plus load and the sill gap and door width are within the robot's limits; the AMR-0600 integrates with lifts and calls idle ones first. Ramps are limited, especially for underride robots that carry the load high; ask for gradeability at full load.
Which safety standard applies to heavy-payload AMRs?
ISO 3691-4 for driverless industrial trucks, and ANSI/A3 R15.08 in North America. The standards are the same as for lighter AMRs, but the risk assessment must be done with the full load: stopping distances, tipping, falling loads and manual recovery.
