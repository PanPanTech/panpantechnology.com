Buying Guide — Outdoor Cleaning Robots

Parking Lot and Parking Garage Sweepers: Ride-On, Truck or Autonomous?

For large surface lots swept on a fixed schedule, an autonomous sweeper can take over the routine night sweep; truck sweepers remain the tool for heavy debris and road-speed contracts, and ride-on or walk-behind sweepers suit small lots with staff on hand. Parking garages are different: low ceilings, ramps, no satellite signal and oil-stained concrete call for compact robots that localise without GNSS, plus a scrubber for the floor itself.

Short answer: match the machine to the site and the schedule

Use an autonomous sweeper where a large paved lot or garage deck is swept on a regular schedule and the job is mainly sand, grit, leaves and litter. Use a truck sweeper for heavy debris, very large contracts or sites spread across a city. Use a walk-behind or ride-on sweeper for small lots where a member of staff is already on site. Add a floor scrubber in garages where oil, tyre marks and dust need washing, not just sweeping.

Parking areas are a good fit for automation because the work is repetitive, it is best done at night when the lot is empty, and the layout — aisles, bays, ramps — changes rarely. The questions that decide the machine are the size of the area, what the debris is, whether the site is open-air or covered, and who is available to operate and empty it.

This guide compares the main options, explains what is different about garages, and shows where our outdoor sweeping robots and the ACR-0800 driverless scrubber fit. For how driverless sweepers work in general, see our guide to autonomous street sweepers.

Parking garage checklist for robot sweepers

In a garage, check four things before anything else: height clearance, ramp gradient, localisation without satellite signal, and how the robot changes level.
Height clearanceMeasure the lowest point on the route — entry barrier, beam, duct or sign. Our outdoor sweepers range from 1,220 mm high (ACR-1200) to 1,710 mm (ACR-1800); the ACR-1300 is 1,280 mm and the ACR-1380 1,520 mm.
RampsCompare the steepest ramp with the robot's rating at full load: about 15° for the ACR-1200, 12° (20 %) for the ACR-1300, 17 % at full load for the ACR-1380. Wet or painted ramps reduce grip.
LocalisationUnderground there is no satellite positioning, so the robot must localise from lidar and cameras. The ACR-1200 is specified for millimetre-level positioning in areas with weak or no GNSS.
Speed humps and drainsCheck step clearance (7 cm on the ACR-1300) and that drain grates and expansion joints are within the robot's limits.
Parked cars and peopleSchedule sweeping when the garage is emptiest, and confirm the robot detects low objects such as wheel stops and kerbs — the ACR-1380 detects obstacles from 10 cm high.
Turning and aislesTight corners and ramps need a small turning radius — 1.35 m on the ACR-1380 — and enough aisle width beside parked cars.

Oil stains and floor scrubbing in garages

Sweeping removes loose debris but not oil drips, tyre marks or the fine dust that turns to grime on concrete and epoxy decks. Garages that want a clean floor — not just a swept one — add periodic scrubbing. A driverless scrubber such as the ACR-0800 sweeps, scrubs and recovers water in one pass on concrete and epoxy, with an 800 mm scrub path, 1,160 mm sweep width and a 140 L clean-water tank, and is rated for indoor and outdoor hard floors.

A common pattern is a robot sweep every night and a scrub once a week or after events, with pressure washing for heavy stains. For more on how scrubbers compare, see autonomous floor scrubbers.

Cost: owning a robot vs a sweeping contract

Many lots are swept by contractors who charge per visit or per month. A robot makes sense when sweeping is frequent — daily or several times a week — and the site is large enough to keep the robot busy. Compare the contract cost, or your own crew hours, with the robot's full cost: machine, charging point, mapping and commissioning, software, insurance, brushes and filters, and the supervision and emptying time that remains.

Property managers with several lots or garages close together can share one robot or schedule several, which improves the return. Contractors can also use robots themselves to cover more sites with the same crew.

FAQ

What is the best way to clean a parking lot?
Sweep regularly to remove sand, grit and litter, pick up large items by hand, and wash or scrub stained areas periodically. Large lots usually use a truck sweeper contract or an autonomous sweeper on a night schedule; small lots use a walk-behind sweeper.
How often should a parking lot be swept?
It depends on traffic and debris. Busy retail and hospital lots are often swept daily or several times a week, quieter office lots weekly. Leaf season, events and winter grit increase the need. Frequent sweeping is where an autonomous sweeper pays off best.
Can a robot sweeper clean a parking garage?
Yes, if it fits under the lowest height clearance, can climb the ramps at full load, and localises without satellite positioning. Compact sweepers suit garage decks; stained concrete also needs periodic scrubbing.
Do autonomous sweepers work around parked cars?
They detect and avoid parked cars, wheel stops and kerbs, but they sweep best when the lot is mostly empty, so most sites schedule them at night or before opening. Bays occupied during the sweep are picked up on the next run.
Can a parking lot sweeper remove oil stains?
No. Sweepers collect loose debris. Oil and fluid stains need a floor scrubber with detergent, or pressure washing for heavy stains.
What height clearance does a robot sweeper need?
At least its own height plus a margin. Our outdoor sweepers are 1,220 mm to 1,710 mm high, so most garage entries and decks are passable, but measure the lowest beam, duct or sign on the actual route.
Is a robot cheaper than a parking lot sweeping contract?
It can be when sweeping is frequent and the site is large; for occasional sweeps a contract is usually cheaper. Compare the contract or crew cost with the robot's full cost, including charging, mapping, software, wear parts and the supervision that remains.
