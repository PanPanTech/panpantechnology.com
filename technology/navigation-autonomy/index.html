How autonomy works on site

From first map to daily routes

Every deployment goes through the same sequence. Knowing it helps facility teams plan the time and access the commissioning engineer needs.

01

Mapping

The robot is driven or walked through the site once and builds a map from LiDAR and camera data. The ACR-1380 commissions a new site in about 10 minutes.

02

Zone and route editing

Cleaning zones, delivery points, no-go areas and speed limits are drawn on the map in software, not marked on the floor.

03

Localisation

During work the robot matches live sensor data to the map to know where it is, even when shelves, pallets or furniture have moved.

04

Obstacle handling

Layered sensors detect people, pallets, glass, low objects and drops. The robot slows, re-plans or stops depending on distance and object type.

05

Docking

Repeatable docking to charging, water and conveyor stations lets robots refill, drain or hand over loads without help.

Design choices

Why sensor fusion beats any single sensor

Each sensor type has blind spots. Fusion means one layer covers what another misses.

LiDAR

Accurate distance and mapping in any light, but can miss glass, very low objects and black surfaces.

Depth and RGB cameras

Recognise objects, stains and people and see low obstacles, but need light and are affected by glare.

Ultrasonic and radar

Detect glass and close obstacles that optical sensors miss; short range only.

Cliff and bumper sensors

Last-line protection against drops, stairs and contact.

IMU

Tracks motion and tilt between sensor updates, which matters on ramps and uneven outdoor surfaces.

On-board computing

AI recognition runs on the robot, so decisions do not wait for a network round trip.

FAQ

Navigation and autonomy questions

Do your robots need magnetic strips, QR codes or reflectors?

No. Robots navigate from a map built with LiDAR and cameras. Routes and zones are edited in software, so layout changes do not require floor markers.

Can robots work around people?

Yes. Layered sensors detect people and obstacles at different heights, and the robots slow down, re-plan or stop. Outdoor models such as the ACR-1330 add automatic emergency braking.

What happens when furniture or pallets move?

Localisation matches live sensor data to the stored map, so moved objects are treated as obstacles rather than causing the robot to get lost. Large permanent changes are handled by updating the map.

Can a robot detect glass walls and doors?

Glass is hard for LiDAR alone, which is why indoor models add ultrasonic, radar or depth-camera layers. Large glass areas can also be marked as virtual walls during commissioning.

How long does it take to map a new site?

It depends on size and complexity. The ACR-1380 outdoor sweeper commissions a new site in about 10 minutes; indoor buildings with many zones take longer because routes and schedules are set up as well.

Do AMRs work with other vendors' fleet managers?

The AMR-0600 supports the VDA5050 protocol, which is designed for mixed-vendor fleets. Other models connect through our dispatch platform and APIs.
