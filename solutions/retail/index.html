Rollout path

From pilot store to chain rollout

Most chains run one or two pilot stores before committing to a region. The steps below are the order we work in, and the information we need from you at each stage.

01

Store survey and label count

Send fixture types, SKU count per store and photos of shelf rails. We return a label mix by size and a base-station count per floor plan.

02

Integration plan

We map your ERP or POS price fields to AiESL and agree whether price pushes come from HQ, the store or both.

03

Pilot store

One store goes live with labels, base stations and, if wanted, one cleaning robot. Staff learn binding, refresh and roll-call on the base station.

04

Measure

Compare price-change labour, label error rate and night-cleaning hours before and after. These numbers drive the rollout business case.

05

Regional rollout

Stores are added by group in the cloud platform with role-based access for HQ, regions and franchisees.

Where it pays back

Retail scenarios we are most often asked about

These are the workflows where shelf-edge automation and cleaning robots replace the most manual hours. Your own numbers come out of the pilot store, not from a brochure.

Weekly promotion changes

Price and promotion swaps that used to need paper tags and a night shift become a scheduled push from HQ, with a roll-call to confirm every label updated.

Fresh and markdown pricing

Larger 4.2" and 7.5" labels carry dynamic markdowns for fresh produce without reprinting, and the audit trail shows who changed what.

Omnichannel picking

Each label carries a 3-colour LED and a CODE128 ID, which can be used to flag SKUs for online-order picking from the same shelf data that drives pricing.

After-hours floor care

A docked cleaning robot runs aisles after closing and logs the route, replacing a nightly mop-and-bucket round.

Daytime cleaning with media

In malls, the ACR-0520M cleans high-traffic floors while playing paid content on its 27-inch screen.

Chain-wide reporting

Footfall from Pyroglaux and label status sit in one data layer, so regions compare stores on the same basis.

FAQ

Retail solution questions

How long does an electronic shelf label battery last?

Classic Series labels are rated at more than five years at four refreshes per day, with replaceable cells. Battery life drops if you refresh more often, so share your promotion frequency and we will estimate it for your schedule.

Do the labels keep working if the store loses internet?

Yes. The base station runs its own business system with labels, products, templates, binding and refresh, so stores can keep updating labels locally while the cloud link is down.

Can the system connect to our existing ERP or POS?

Yes. AiESL is the integration layer and offers documented APIs for two-way sync of price, product and stock data. We agree the field mapping during the integration plan, before the pilot store goes live.

Which cleaning robot suits a supermarket?

For small stores and convenience formats, the 22.3 kg ACR-0290 with its self-service station. For full supermarket aisles, the ACR-0440P (500–650 m²/h) or ACR-0520 (about 1,197 m²/h scrubbing). Malls that want to sell screen time can use the ACR-0520M.

How many stores use the platform today?

The group's shelf-label and data platform is deployed in more than 6,000 stores through its retail brands.

Can we start with one store?

Yes, and we recommend it. A pilot store gives you measured labour savings and label accuracy before you commit a region, and it lets your staff learn the base-station workflow.
