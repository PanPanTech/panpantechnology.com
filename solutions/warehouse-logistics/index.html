Deployment path

How a warehouse fleet goes live

AMRs and scrubbers share aisles with forklifts and pickers, so the work is mostly in routes, handoff points and traffic rules. This is the order we run a project in.

01

Site data

Send a layout drawing, aisle widths, payloads, rack dimensions, shift pattern and peak flows. For floor care, add floor finish and soil type.

02

Route and handoff design

We mark pick-up and drop-off points, charging spots, one-way aisles and forklift crossings, and size the fleet against peak throughput.

03

Mapping and commissioning

Robots map the site with LiDAR and vision; routes are edited in software, so no magnetic tape or QR grid is laid.

04

Integration

Connect dispatch to WMS or MES, elevators and doors. AMR-0600 supports VDA5050 for multi-vendor fleets.

05

Pilot and scale

Run one route or zone for two to four weeks, measure trips per hour and interventions, then add robots and zones.

Sizing checklist

What we need to quote a warehouse fleet

A fleet proposal is only as good as the site data behind it. These are the inputs that change robot count and model choice the most.

Payload and load carrier

Weight, footprint and whether loads sit on racks, trolleys, totes or pallets. This decides between top-carry, underride lifting and towing.

Aisle and door widths

Minimum clear width on every route, including doors and lifts. AMR-0300 needs about 600 mm path clearance; underride models need room under the rack.

Throughput at peak

Moves per hour at the busiest shift, not the average. Robot count is sized to peak, with charging time factored in.

Floor condition

Slopes, thresholds, expansion joints and floor finish affect both AMR payload and scrubber choice.

Systems to connect

WMS, MES, elevators, automatic doors and call buttons. Each interface is scoped before the pilot.

Cleaning frequency and soil

Square metres per night, dust versus liquid spills, and forklift tyre marks decide between sweeping, vacuuming and scrubbing.

FAQ

Warehouse solution questions

Do your AMRs need magnetic tape or QR codes on the floor?

No. The AMR range navigates with LiDAR SLAM, and the AMR-0300 adds visual SLAM. Routes are drawn in software, so changing a layout does not mean relaying floor markers.

What is the difference between AMR-0600 and AMR-0600R?

Both carry 600 kg. AMR-0600 comes as a mast version with a built-in screen or a 255 mm underride version, and supports VDA5050. AMR-0600R is a 365 mm underride lifting robot with three LiDARs and a 50 mm lift stroke, rated for −10 to +65 °C.

Can the robots work alongside forklifts and people?

Yes, but traffic rules matter more than sensors. We design one-way aisles, crossing points and speed zones during route design, and the AMR-0300 is built to ISO 3691-4.

Can an AMR use elevators?

AMR-0300 supports elevator control, e-door access and call buttons, so it can deliver across floors once the elevator interface is connected.

Which scrubber suits a warehouse?

ACR-0600 for unattended aisle cleaning with its 5-in-1 dock, up to 2,160 m²/h. ACR-0800 for very large dock and staging floors, up to 4,000 m²/h. Our guide on industrial robot vacuums for warehouses covers dust and debris cases.

Should we choose an AMR or an AGV?

If routes change or people share the aisles, an AMR is usually the better fit. Our AMR vs AGV guide compares the two on infrastructure, cost and flexibility.
