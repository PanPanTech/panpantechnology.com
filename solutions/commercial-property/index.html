Deployment path

How a building maintenance fleet is planned

Indoor robots, facade robots and outdoor sweepers have different setup work. We plan them together so one contact covers the building and the reporting runs through one fleet cloud.

01

Building survey

Floor plans, floor finishes, cleaning hours and tenant rules for indoor areas; facade material, height, panel grid and roof anchors for exterior work.

02

Zone plan

Split the building into robot zones, manual zones and timing windows, such as lobbies off-peak and corridors overnight.

03

Facade method statement

Anchor positions or counterweights, drop sequence, wind limits and manual touch-up areas are agreed before mobilisation.

04

Commissioning

Indoor robots are mapped and stations installed; facade crews are trained at the factory or on site.

05

Reporting

Coverage maps and completion rates per zone go into tenant reports and service-level reviews.

Sizing checklist

What to send for a building proposal

These are the details that change model choice and fleet size for a commercial building. Partial answers are fine; we fill gaps during the survey.

Indoor floor area by type

Square metres of lobby stone, corridor carpet, office vinyl and car-park concrete, and how often each is cleaned.

Cleaning windows

When tenants are in, when the building is empty, and any areas that must be cleaned during the day.

Water and power points

Where a station can connect to water and drain, which decides whether ACR-0520P can run unattended.

Facade details

Building height, facade material, panel grid, raised frames and photos of the roof edge.

Roof capacity

Existing anchors and their rating, or whether counterweights or a gondola would be needed.

Outdoor routes

Plaza and road area, narrowest passage, kerbs and where collected waste is emptied.

FAQ

Commercial property questions

How high can your facade cleaning robots work?

The AFR-1500 is rated for buildings up to 300 m working height with a 150 m rope as standard and longer rope on a project basis. The AFR-1200 suits lighter, faster setups, and the AFR-0400 climbs concrete and steel by vacuum adhesion.

How much facade can one robot clean per day?

Plan with 1,500–2,000 m² per AFR-1500 per day, from manufacturer project records. The 180–500 m²/h ratings are peaks on continuous facade and do not include rigging or drop changes.

What happens if a facade robot loses power?

The AFR-1500 rope-climbing system locks automatically and holds position. It also stops and holds on adhesion loss, over-speed, over-load or attitude deviation, with alarms on the robot and the ground panel.

Can cleaning robots work during office hours?

Yes. The ACR-0350 runs below 60 dB and fits 60 cm aisles, and the ACR-0520P has quiet sweep-vacuum modes. Most buildings schedule lobbies off-peak and corridors after hours.

Which robot cleans car parks and plazas?

The ACR-1380 sweeps, vacuums, blows and suppresses dust with mist and carries a 150 L hopper. The ACR-1300 folds to 1,050 mm for back lanes and footpaths, and the ACR-1200 runs 6–8 hours on large open areas.

Can we see tenant-facing cleaning reports?

The fleet cloud records coverage maps, completion rates and water and power use per zone, which property managers use as evidence in service-level and tenant reviews.
